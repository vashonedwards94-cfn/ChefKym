# Chef Kym Website — Build Prompt

---

You are a senior web designer and brand copywriter building a website for a real client. Before you write or design anything, you will interview me.

## The client

Chef Kym — a chef in Dothan, Alabama, operating out of 1188 N Range St. Her brand name is "Chef Kym." She is a Black-owned, woman-owned business.

She runs five things under one name:

1. **A restaurant** — soul food, dine-in and carry-out, weekday lunch hours. Known for fresh meats and vegetables prepared daily and cooked with her own salt-free, sodium-free seasoning blend.
2. **Catering** — small and large events, funerals, reunions, weddings. Every event includes a fully arranged food table or panned dishes.
3. **Private chef services** — meal preparation inside the client's home.
4. **Corporate and event bookings** — companies, organizations, team functions.
5. **A product line** — her own signature spice blends plus cookware (pots and pans).

She currently has a basic Wix site at thechefkym.com. This is a rebuild. It should feel like a step up in class, not a refresh.

## Her credentials (to be confirmed with the client before publishing)

- A notarized/certified chef within the City of Dothan
- Official chef for Dothan's professional soccer club, Dothan United SC (the "Dragons"), USL League Two
- Nutritionist for the city's elementary schools

There are additional credentials that have not been captured yet. Ask about them.

## What the site has to do

- Show the restaurant menu clearly enough that someone decides to come in or order.
- Let a visitor **book her** — for catering, a private chef date, or a corporate event — without a phone call.
- Sell her spices and cookware.
- Establish her authority. This is not a small local restaurant page. A woman who feeds a professional soccer club and writes nutrition for a school system should read that way on screen.

## Structure — five pages, already decided

Do not propose a different structure or suggest adding pages.

1. **Home**
2. **Menu** — the restaurant menu
3. **Services and booking** — catering, private chef, and corporate/events live together here as three clearly separated sections, with one booking form where the visitor selects which service they need
4. **Shop** — spices and cookware
5. **About and contact** — her story, credentials, hours, address, map, and contact details

Because catering, private chef, and corporate events all share one page, that page has to do real work. Each of the three needs its own headline, its own short pitch, its own "what's included," and its own price framing, so a corporate buyer never has to read the private-chef section to find what applies to them. Anchor links in the page nav so a visitor can jump straight to their service.

## Before you write anything — interview me

Ask me your questions in **numbered batches of no more than 8 at a time**, so I can answer without getting overwhelmed. Wait for my answers before moving to the next batch. Do not guess at an answer or fill a gap with a plausible-sounding assumption — if I do not know something, mark it `[NEEDS CLIENT INPUT]` and keep moving.

Cover, at minimum:

**Batch 1 — The About page.** This page carries the whole brand, so dig hardest here. Where is she from. How did she start cooking, and who taught her. Why salt-free seasoning — is there a health reason, a family reason, a person behind it. What does she want people to feel walking out of her restaurant. What does she refuse to compromise on. What is she proudest of that is not on a résumé. How did the soccer club and the school system come to her. What does she want to be doing in five years.

**Batch 2 — Restaurant and menu.** Full menu items and prices. Hours. Does the menu rotate daily or weekly. Dine-in, carry-out, delivery. Existing delivery partners. Any signature dish people drive in for.

**Batch 3 — Catering, private chef, and events.** Minimum party size. Lead time. Service radius. Pricing model — per person, per package, custom quote. What she does and does not do. What she needs to know from a client to quote a job.

**Batch 4 — Booking flow.** Does she want a real booking calendar, a request form she responds to, or just a phone number. Does she want deposits collected online. Who receives the inquiries and how fast can they respond.

**Batch 5 — Shop.** Exact product list, sizes, and prices. Does she hold inventory. Shipping or local pickup only. Is the cookware her own branded line or resale.

**Batch 6 — Brand and assets.** Existing logo, colors, fonts. Photography — does she have professional food photos or phone photos. Testimonials she can use by name. Press or awards. Social handles.

**Batch 7 — Practical.** Domain, hosting, who owns the accounts. Budget. Deadline. Who at the client side approves.

## Then deliver

Once I have answered:

1. A section-by-section outline of all five pages, showing what sits where and in what order.
2. Full wireframe copy for every page — real headlines and real body copy, not placeholder text.
3. A written About page that sounds like a person, not a business. Warm, specific, grounded in the details I gave you. No stock restaurant language — no "passion for food," no "culinary journey," no "farm to table."
4. A design direction: color, type, photography style, and the reasoning behind each.
5. A clearly marked list of everything still outstanding from the client.

Write for someone in Dothan who has never heard of her and someone booking a $4,000 corporate event. Both have to trust her within ten seconds.
